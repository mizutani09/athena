# Paper collapse AMR+1 validation (2026-11-21)

## Purpose and provenance

This run closes the remaining resolution check for the rotating, weak-field
table-EOS collapse application.  It starts from the immutable cycle-1780
checkpoint of the 32.25 microG trajectory, adds one AMR level, and advances to
the same `rho_max >= 1e-10 g cm^-3` density regime as the prior lower-resolution
result.  It is a resolution check of a first-core *density candidate*, not a
standalone proof of hydrostatic first-core formation.

## Additional central-structure diagnostics

The final high-resolution HDF was also analyzed without launching another
simulation.  The diagnostic subtracts the mass-weighted bulk velocity inside
5 au and measures shell averages about the density maximum.  It finds a
volume-weighted inner transition from supersonic to subsonic infall at
`6.738 au`, consistent with the 6--7 au first-core-candidate scale identified
in the earlier literature comparison.  The enclosed mass at that transition
is `0.02045 Msun`.

The transition is not spherical.  At the same radius the mass-weighted
`-v_r/sqrt(P/rho)` proxy is only `0.361`, even though its volume-weighted value
crosses unity.  Low-density directions therefore carry the supersonic inflow,
while most of the shell mass is already subsonic.  This is a more appropriate
interpretation for the rotating, magnetized calculation than claiming one
spherical accretion shock.

For 1--4 au, the median scalar outward-support proxy is `0.865` of spherical
monopole gravity, of which gas pressure supplies `0.777`.  At the sonic
transition the scalar sum is `1.301`; gas pressure and centrifugal support
contribute `0.727` and `0.439`, respectively.  These values demonstrate a
strongly decelerated and partially supported center, but do not prove exact
hydrostatic equilibrium.  The proxy omits magnetic tension, uses `E_r/3`
instead of the unavailable local FLD limiter, and replaces the full gravity
field by the enclosed-mass monopole.

The candidate core has mass-weighted plasma-beta median `8.81` and tenth
percentile `2.70`.  Only `1.95%` of its mass, but `22.2%` of its volume, has
beta below unity.  Magnetic pressure is therefore important in lower-density
channels without dominating most of the central mass.

Temperature anisotropy separates cleanly into physical-axis and Cartesian
components.  Between 1 au and the transition, the polar/equatorial shell
temperature ratio differs from unity by as much as `36.8%`, while the
equatorial Cartesian-axis/diagonal ratio differs by at most `0.825%`.
Consequently the large directional temperature contrast is dominated by the
rotating/magnetized structure; the measured equatorial grid imprint is below
one percent at this resolution.

Running the identical diagnostic on the lower-resolution matched-density
snapshot gives a transition radius change of only `-0.079%`, enclosed core
mass `-1.14%`, inner scalar-support proxy `-2.48%`, and beta median `-1.58%`
on AMR+1.  The physical polar/equatorial temperature contrast changes from
35.2% to 36.8%, while the equatorial axis/diagonal contrast decreases from
2.19% to 0.825%.  Thus both the central structure and its physical anisotropy
are stable to the added AMR level, whereas the Cartesian component decreases
with resolution.

## Decision and artifacts

The additional AMR level doubles the Jeans margin at the target density while
changing the central thermodynamic and enclosed-mass diagnostics by at most
about 1.5%.  This passes the intended resolution-sensitivity gate for a paper
application demonstrating stable table-EOS, variable-opacity, rotating and
magnetized collapse into the first-core density regime.  No further collapse
run is required for that scoped numerical claim.  A stronger statement that a
time-stationary hydrostatic first core has formed would still require temporal
force balance with the full magnetic tension and the actual FLD limiter.
